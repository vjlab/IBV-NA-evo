## Abstract
There is increasing interest in influenza vaccines that contain both haemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA), requiring an understanding of NA antigenic drift. While the antigenic evolution of the influenza A virus NA has been investigated, the antigenic properties of the influenza B virus (IBV) NA remain uncharacterised. To address this, we assessed IBV NA antigenic evolution between 1940-2021, combining antigenic cartography using ferret antisera and characterisation using human monoclonal antibodies, with mutagenesis studies to determine the antigenic impact of mutations. The antigenic evolution of the IBV HA and NA were discordant, with no separation of the IBV NA into the two antigenic lineages. Antigenic mutations of the IBV NA are scattered across the NA lateral and top surfaces, with the active site remaining highly conserved. These insights into IBV NA antigenic evolution can facilitate antigen selection for next generation influenza vaccines by supporting sequence-based surveillance of influenza virus antigenic evolution.
